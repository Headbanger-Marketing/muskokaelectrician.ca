Before work begins

Work out where the problem starts before you call.

Is your property empty for weeks between stays? If so, a flicker or dead circuit may be noticed only on arrival, so note what you found and whether neighbours saw the same. In a home lived in daily, such notes can still help.

Photograph the panel door and any labels from the outside, and note who last visited and what they saw. Those details help the contractor decide whether the cause sits with the supply or with the wiring.

How can I tell a supply interruption from a fault in my wiring?

Check whether neighbouring properties also lost power, and whether the whole property or only some circuits were affected. A shared pattern may point to the supply, while a single dead area is a reason to ask the contractor to assess the property wiring.

Should a panel upgrade wait until I know what failed?

A supply loss and a faulty circuit call for different work, so the cause should be established first. Ask the contractor to say in writing whether the panel was part of the problem or simply affected by it.

How do I compare quotes when the property is empty for weeks?

Compare what each quote says about visit timing, access and who holds the key, as well as the scope itself. A quote that assumes someone is present may not fit a property visited only now and then.

Who needs to be there when the contractor visits?

Someone who can authorize the work and open the door should be named before the visit. If you are away, ask the contractor what can be assessed from outside and what needs access to the inside.

Once I send the details, who confirms anything about the visit?

Not this site. It publishes planning guides and reviews the enquiries sent in, but sets up no visit, quotes no price and takes on no electrical work. Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you deal with must confirm its identity and licence separately, and the job, cost and dates directly with you. Check that licence with ESA.

An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Muskoka property

List every worn, loose or two-prong receptacle by room, noting which are in daily use and, if the property is not lived in all year, which sit idle between visits. Photograph each faceplate from the front, without removing it.

Ask the contractor whether a two-prong receptacle can be replaced on its own or whether the circuit behind it needs assessment, and how the work will be described in the written scope.

What should the assessment establish?

Whether worn, loose or two-prong, each outlet reviewed at the Muskoka property is listed with the condition it was found in. If a replacement can be fitted on the existing circuit, the report says so; if not, it names the other work needed first. The contractor records what each receptacle serves, including any that feeds several rooms. The report separates the items replaced from those left, giving a reason for each. It also names what to watch for afterwards, such as warmth or a loose fit, and who to contact.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a plug adapter fix a two-prong outlet properly?

An adapter does not supply the missing connection, so it should not be treated as a fix. Ask the contractor to assess the circuit and propose a suitable replacement, and avoid plugging in sensitive equipment meanwhile.

What does it mean when a plug feels warm or falls out of the outlet?

It can mean a worn receptacle, a poor connection or an overloaded circuit, and only an assessment can tell which. Stop using that outlet, do not force the plug and describe what you noticed to the contractor.

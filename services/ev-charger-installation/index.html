The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Muskoka property

Collect the tenancy terms about alterations, note the parking spot and the distance to the panel in words, and photograph the meter and the closed panel door. Write down who holds the keys.

Ask the contractor who must sign the work order, whether the landlord has to approve the circuit in writing, and what happens to the equipment when the tenancy ends.

What should the assessment establish?

Responsibility for the circuit and for the equipment has a line naming each party, which matters where a tenant charges at a Muskoka rental the landlord owns. A finding on the existing supply states whether it can carry the charger alongside the other loads. The contractor's list of approvals includes the ESA notification and says who files it. For tenant and landlord alike, one section records where the charger is mounted and what becomes of it when the tenancy ends. A note on charging interruptions explains how one is investigated, so a supply event is not mistaken for a charger fault.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Who has to agree before a rented property gets a charger?

Usually the tenancy terms and the building owner decide that, not the contractor. Get the approval in writing and give a copy to the contractor so the scope reflects what was agreed.

How do I tell a supply drop from a charger fault when it stops?

Note the time, whether other items in the property also lost power and what the charger display showed. Do not keep restarting it. Give those notes to the contractor, who can assess the cause.

Useful project details

  • Vehicle and charger model if chosen
  • Parking position and preferred equipment location
  • Indoor or outdoor conditions
  • Existing supply records if available
  • Other major loads and future charging needs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
